## Episode concept (sample)

**Episode 1 — What a first session actually feels like**

A seated, unhurried conversation about the hour someone finally stops and sits down. Danny welcomes people of every faith, and of none. He talks through what a first session is, and what it isn’t: a real conversation, a pause to discern, spiritual and personal insight — not a performance, not a diagnosis, and not a promise that a specific message will come through.

If a guest is present, they speak from their own life (grief, calling, or a first experience of being truly heard). If it is a solo sitting, Danny teaches from the same outline and leaves space for a few listener questions gathered in advance.

The tone is the same as a session: no rush, no script to perform. Sign-off: *Blessings and love.*

## Run-of-show (45–60 minutes)

| Time | Segment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–2:00 | Open and welcome | Who this sitting is for. All faiths, and none. Today is a conversation, not a performance. |
| 2:00–8:00 | What *un momentico* means | The Cuban-home pause: sit down, look at me, be here. English first; a Spanish line if he is comfortable. |
| 8:00–18:00 | What a first session is, and isn’t | No script. No rush. Scheduled for one hour, with buffer. “Your session ends when you’re ready, not when the clock says.” |
| 18:00–30:00 | Listening in hard moments | His path, in the terms he has confirmed: years of listening, then this work. No invented stories. Guest (if any) reflects on a time they needed to be heard. |
| 30:00–42:00 | Grief, and loved ones | Gentle, no timeline, no guarantees. Mediumship is held with care, only in wording he has signed off. |
| 42:00–52:00 | One honest next step | Not ten. A discernment question to sit with this week. If it is a Q&A format, this block is listener questions instead. |
| 52:00–58:00 | Close | How to stay close (follow, newsletter once it exists, booking once it is live). *Blessings and love.* |
| 58:00–60:00 | Hold / credits | Quiet end card. Disclaimer on screen: for spiritual and personal insight; not a substitute for medical, psychological, legal, or financial advice. |

Times are a guide, not a script. If a story needs more room, let it have it.

## Guest-type ideas (no real names)

These are types, not people to contact yet. Any real guest needs a named sign-off before outreach.

1. **A faith or community guide** — someone who already holds space for people in transition, and is comfortable sitting with a spiritual conversation that welcomes every faith, and none.
2. **Someone who has walked through grief and is ready to talk about living with it** — not a current client, unless that person has given written permission to be invited.
3. **A wellness practitioner whose work sits beside his** — life-purpose, contemplative, or pastoral care. Not clinical mental health care, and not a medical practice.

## Recording-setup guide (for Danny)

Keep it simple. This should feel like a session, not a production.

- A quiet room with warm, even light — a window to the side is enough. Sit; don’t stand.
- Two chairs at a slight angle, so it is a conversation, not an interview desk. For a solo sitting, one chair, camera at eye level.
- One stable camera (a phone on a tripod is fine) plus a second phone for a backup close-up, if someone can start it and leave it.
- A small lavalier or a decent USB microphone, if he has one. If not, a phone close to the speaker is better than a camera mic across the room.
- On-screen: a short title card, his name, and the working show name. Captions in the edit.
- Before recording: phones on silent, a glass of water, a printed run-of-show with the few lines he wants to hit, not a script to read.
- After recording: we handle the edit, the clip map, and the draft package. Nothing goes live until he has signed off.

Comfort on camera is still to confirm. The first sitting can be shorter if that helps him settle.
