Hola, and welcome.

This is the first note from Un Momentico. If you're reading it, you either know me, or someone thought you might need a pause. Either way, I'm grateful you're here.

In a Cuban home, when someone says *"un momentico,"* you stop what you're doing. It never meant "just a second." It meant: sit down, look at me, be here. That's what I want these notes to be. A few quiet minutes in a week that probably hasn't been quiet.

Each issue will have three small things: something about how this work actually happens, one question to sit with, and a word for anyone who's carrying something heavy.

## What a first session actually feels like

People tell me they were nervous before their first session. They didn't know what to say, or whether they had to believe something to come in.

Here's the truth: you don't have to prepare anything.

We slow down. You talk, I listen. Some people come with one clear question. Some come with a feeling they can't name yet. Both are welcome. There's no script, and there's nothing to perform.

I'm open about the spirit, energy and faith that guide my own life. That's my story. It isn't a requirement for yours. People of every faith, and of none, sit with me. The only thing I ask is that you arrive open.

Sessions are scheduled for one hour, and I always leave extra time after. I don't believe in cutting someone off in the middle of something important. **Your session ends when you're ready, not when the clock says.**

Most people leave with one thing to sit with, and one small step for the week. Not ten. One.

## This week's question

> **Where in my life am I staying busy so I don't have to sit with what my heart already knows?**

Try this: find five quiet minutes. Phone face down. Three slow breaths. Read the question twice, then write whatever comes first, without editing it.

Then ask a second, smaller question: *Am I moving from fear, or from love?*

You don't have to act on everything you write. Pick one honest step and take it before the week is over. Discern, then proceed with more light and love.

## For anyone carrying grief

If someone you love has passed, I want you to hear this clearly: you don't have to be "over it" to keep living, and you don't have to carry it alone.

Grief doesn't follow a timeline. It shows up in a song at the grocery store, or on a Tuesday that should have been ordinary. That's not you doing grief wrong. That's love with nowhere to go.

In some sessions I offer medium guidance: a respectful opening to receive messages from loved ones who have passed, when that gift is present. I hold it with care and humility, and I never promise a specific message or outcome. Sometimes the most important part of the hour is simply talking about the person: their laugh, their habits, the way they said your name.

If that's where you are, this space is open to you. And please keep leaning on your doctor, your counselor and your people too. This work sits alongside them, not in place of them.
